Visas & Entry Requirements

Yes, a valid tourist visa is required for most foreign travelers entering Tanzania. Your passport must be valid for at least six months from your planned date of departure. A standard single-entry tourist visa is valid for up to 3 months from the date of issue. While you can purchase a visa on arrival at major entry points (like Kilimanjaro International Airport, Julius Nyerere International Airport in Dar es Salaam, and Zanzibar Airport), we highly recommend applying for an e-Visa online at least two weeks before your trip to avoid long queues at the border.

Most travelers do not require a Yellow Fever vaccination. The World Health Organization classifies Tanzania as a low-risk country. However, if you are arriving from or transiting through a country with a risk of Yellow Fever transmission (for more than 12 hours), you will be required to show proof of vaccination (a Yellow Card) upon arrival.

Yes. The Government of Zanzibar requires all international visitors to hold mandatory inbound travel insurance. The policy, exclusively offered by the Zanzibar Insurance Corporation (ZIC), costs USD $44 per adult and USD $22 for children aged 2–17. It must be secured via the official portal before your arrival on the island and covers specific local medical emergencies, evacuations, and travel disruptions during your stay.

The Zanzibar Infrastructure Tax is a mandatory government fee ranging from USD $1 to $5 per person, per night, depending on the hotel’s official star rating. This fee is not included in your safari package and must be paid directly to your resort or hotel upon checkout in Zanzibar to support local infrastructure development.

Safari Planning & Experience

Tanzania is a rewarding year-round destination, but your ideal travel window depends on your goals:

Dry Season (June to October): Best for overall wildlife viewing and witnessing the Great Wildebeest Migration, as vegetation is thin and animals gather around natural water sources.

Green Season (November to May): Excellent for birdwatching, lush green landscapes, newborn wildlife, and fewer crowds.

Yes, Tanzania is recognized as one of the safest and most politically stable countries in Africa. At Liongate Adventures, your safety is our absolute priority. Our professional guides are highly experienced in navigating both the wilderness and local towns safely. As with any travel destination, we recommend taking standard common-sense precautions, securing your valuables, and adhering closely to your guide’s safety instructions in the national parks.

We recommend packing lightweight, breathable clothing in neutral tones (such as khaki, olive green, beige, or brown) to blend into the environment and avoid attracting insects.

Essentials: A warm jacket or fleece for cool early-morning game drives, a wide-brimmed hat, comfortable closed-toe walking shoes, sunglasses, and high-SPF sunscreen. Avoid bright white clothing (which gets dusty easily) and dark blue or black clothing (which can attract tsetse flies in certain areas).

Tanzania offers some of the densest and most spectacular concentrations of wildlife on earth. Depending on the parks you visit (such as the Serengeti, Ngorongoro Crater, or Tarangire), you can expect to encounter the "Big Five" (lion, leopard, elephant, buffalo, and rhino), alongside vast herds of wildebeest and zebras, giraffes, cheetahs, hippos, Nile crocodiles, and hundreds of vibrant native bird species.

Tipping is not mandatory, but it is deeply appreciated and customary within the East African tourism industry as a way to thank your team for exceptional service. As a general guideline, many guests tip:

Safari Driver-Guide: USD $15 to $25 per day, per group.

Mountain Crew (Kilimanjaro): USD $10 to $15 per day, per group for your main guide, with scaled adjustments for assistant guides, camp cooks, and porters.
